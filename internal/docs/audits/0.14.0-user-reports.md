The installed PP100 reports SAT 8801, Kernel/Normal ID76 and revision 1.00.
Its captured Normal hardware family is `cf3183a51bc41e42`. The target package
is SAT 8801/ID89, BDX-PR1MLA 1.04, family `31ce81c2dbb0fdb`, SHA-256
`13d89487a90c0e49dab81015f4f18e57e870092d4e73f9a4449559cde4578b9e`.
The local hoard catalog independently places those model/revision variants in
the reported groups. SAT equality alone is not family equality.

Both backup attempts read and verify Normal length `0x1b4200` at `0x410000`
using the Kernel-derived layout. The ordered hashes and sizes of all 114 large
response chunks are identical between standalone and pre-flash capture.
The standalone TAR is saved and read back successfully, SHA-256
`da6ecd26d7e1fc4d60a1478cdb97ec500fa01fb87edc56ff0a680850d928d875`.
This is evidence of reliable capture in this session, not a proven restore.

There are zero data-out payload commands in every included operation. Each
Pioneer backup sends 117 non-data `3b 02 41 a5 aa aa 00 00 00 00` read-unlock
knocks; these must not be mistaken for firmware programming based on opcode
`3b` alone. No update-entry, firmware transfer, commit or post-flash startup is
logged. No report demonstrates successful flashing or a damaged drive.

The family refusal is the expected ordinary Flash policy. These reports do not
justify bypassing it, prove physical incompatibility independently of the family
analysis, or identify a replacement receiver protocol. Captured images would be
needed to investigate the underlying configuration differences further.
