# Adversarial review: G03 control field

Verdict: **UHD necessity unproven**, for both detection and physical reading. The observed instruction difference is real and conditionally changes a controller word; neither the identity of that field nor a causal path from its value to UHD detection or successful reading has been established. Do not describe G03 as a proven UHD gate or use the corpus classification as physical-read evidence. Retain it as a reference-alignment candidate, with its hardware effect and required conditions explicit. This review does not show that changing it is unnecessary either.

Evidence uses Thumb image offsets in the hash-pinned images. Earliest BP50 1.00 (`c88a69013d557b9c4cf1a4d603c314585f51a258b9747c345184e866eef36306`) at `0x33a14` loads the state byte from literal `0x01ff9def` at `0x33aa4`. At `0x33a18–0x33a1c`, it computes `0xc8 & ~state`; C7 makes that nonzero, so execution branches to `0x33ad6`. C7 has high bits 3, causing `0x33ade–0x33ae6` to clear bits 7:4 and add `0x30`, before the store at `0x33afc`. The state value C8 takes `0x33a1e–0x33a24`, clearing the same nibble. These comparisons show a state-dependent distinction, not its physical meaning.

The harness's caller-supplied R1 needs qualification: within the real routine R1 is established at `0x339a8–0x339aa` by loading literal `0x04208200` at `0x33a70` and adding `0x40`; the field store at `0x33afc` is `[r1,#0x30]`, address **0x04208270**. Therefore the isolated fragment's R1 stands in for a concrete hardware-mapped address, rather than proving how the full caller obtains it. The register's documented function and impact are unknown. At `0x339fa–0x339fc`, a nonzero R0 bypasses this block to `0x33ad4`; calls at `0x33e0e` and `0x34246` supply extracted bits, and the routine can itself alter R0 at `0x3398a–0x3398e`. C7 state alone does not prove the field write runs during acquisition or reading. The two static BL call sites were found by scanning halfword-aligned Thumb BL encodings; indirect callers and full runtime reachability were not ruled out.

Stock BU40N 1.02 (`1bbeb5351dc0d69feaeefdac9ada7f3f48eed0ca4e2f879d9266679cec133396`) at `0x35442–0x3545e` explicitly compares C7 at `0x35456`, taking the clear-nibble path at `0x3545a`; its conditional context is broadly similar but a matching local sequence does not prove equivalent hardware or runtime state. Old BN11 (`5c01c1fda59f3db130bd14aa64fe97d4010376798716b16792dbffd59c488a95`) already contains the same explicit C7 branch at `0x3541e–0x3543a`. Thus the C7 field behavior is not a discriminator between older and later firmware generally. The existing 23 other older reviewed matches and 147 newer reviewed matches are code observations, not verified physical UHD outcomes; their independence and hardware equivalence are unproven. The result applies directly to these two disassembled old images and the stock reference; other recognized `classic`, `modern`, and `modern_redundant_c8_test` layouts have distinct masked signatures in `uhd_layouts.rs` and need separate contextual verification. A byte-pattern match and isolated helper emulation cannot prove whole-program reachability, absence of false matches outside the bounded recognizer, or hardware semantics.

For a causal finding, trace the actual disc-identification transition into C7 and runtime values at both callers through the full initialization/read path, establish the 0x04208270 register meaning or observe it on real hardware, and compare otherwise identical firmware with only this field selection changed across UHD detection and data reads. Existing full old conversion plans are blocked at G05, so they do not provide a valid partial-conversion outcome for this experiment.

## Downstream register use and candidate boundary

A bounded static literal-and-alias scan (`/tmp/freemkv-g03-scan.py` and `/tmp/freemkv-g03-alias.py`) searched aligned literals 0x04208000–0x04208270 and short straight-line Thumb address propagation. It found no direct 0x04208270 literal in these three images, but did find repeated 0x04208200 and 0x04208240 bases. This is **not an exhaustive enumeration**: computed bases, values propagated across branches or calls, unaligned literals, ARM code, and indirect accesses can escape it. The following accesses are concrete examples, not a claim that no others exist.

* In earliest BP50, `0x27826–0x2782e` reads and writes the word while clearing bit 16; `0x339ac–0x339b4` clears bit 24 before the selector. The G03 nibble store at `0x33afc` is followed for C7 by a separate read-modify-write at `0x33b06–0x33b1e` that sets bit 10. That operation remains on the same native continuation after a C7 selector change.
* BP50 `0x31b42–0x31b4a` saves the entire word on the stack. At `0x31bb4–0x31bb8`, it temporarily writes `r5 << 17`, whose low 17 bits, including bits 7:4, are zero. Following a loop, `0x31c98–0x31ca4` restores the saved word. The corresponding save/overwrite/restore is at `0x33526`, `0x3359c`, `0x33688` in old BN11, and `0x3354a`, `0x335c0`, `0x336ac` in stock BU40N. Whether hardware samples the field while overwritten, and whether an interrupt, reset, or alternate exit changes restoration, are unestablished.
* BN11 has another explicit field RMW at `0x2994e–0x29962`: it clears bits 7:4 and writes `0xc0` there. This demonstrates that even firmware which chooses zero for C7 has other code capable of changing the field. The exact guard and temporal relationship of that operation to disc reads have not been proved; it cannot be presumed to run afterward. Additional BN11 RMW sites include `0x299bc–0x299c6` and `0x29a3c–0x29a46`, whose masks require their full calling context before attributing effects.

The proposed candidate intercepts BP50 `0x33a1a–0x33a1c` (BICS and conditional branch). The implementation's feature-on and C7 case directs execution to the existing native clear path `0x33a1e`, which reads the word, clears only `0xf0`, and stores it at `0x33afc`. Compared with the original C7 path, this preserves all other bits **at that particular RMW**, subject to hardware register read/write semantics; subsequent bit-10 handling remains native. The selector stub saves/restores R3, reconstructs C/V, replays BICS, and returns to a native continuation; static review and earlier isolated CPU tests support the local register/flags contract. They do not constitute an execution of both real callers or prove behavior of MMIO reads, side effects, asynchronous hardware, or the entire firmware. The available conversion plan remains blocked at G05, so no actual full-image G03-only installation was tested here.

BN11 and the other reviewed old matches establish that their instruction encodings can select zero for C7; they do not establish that zero is necessary, sufficient, safe across hardware variants, or reachable during a UHD read. No concrete datasheet or code evidence establishes the name or physical effect of bits 7:4. A minimal experimental change is to reuse the native clear path only for C7 under the existing feature guard, but whether that change works remains unproven. The next discriminating test is an instrumented, otherwise identical G03-only comparison through real acquisition and reading, recording caller arguments and writes to this MMIO word, with hardware observation of detection and returned sectors. An emulated real-caller trace can establish conditional software reachability first, but cannot replace the hardware observation.

### Who reads the saved word

The early BP50 owner begins at `0x31b34`, saving R4–R7/LR and allocating a 0x3c-byte frame. The read at `0x31b42` stores the whole word to `[sp,#0x38]` at `0x31b4a`. The only load of that frame slot in this routine is `0x31c9c`, feeding the unmodified restore at `0x31ca4`: software does **not** extract bits 7:4 from that saved value or use them in its arithmetic. If entered after G03 with no intervening writer and normal completion, either nibble 0 or 3 is saved, temporarily replaced by 0 at `0x31bb8`, and restored to the same saved nibble; these are conditional statements about firmware execution, not established hardware timing or an MMIO behavioral guarantee.

After the temporary write the routine calls `0x5dd3c` with 0x32, then executes a 128-iteration loop `0x31be0–0x31c92` selecting values 0, 3, 6, 9 in another register and calling `0x5dd1a` with 0xc8 four times per iteration. It reads another register, accumulates signed bytes, subtracts sums, and returns a packed result at `0x31d42–0x31d48`. It also calls `0x80254` immediately before the temporary write. This supports describing it as a repeated sampling/measurement-like routine; the physical quantity and the callees' full semantics are not identified. BN11 begins the analogous routine at `0x33518`, and stock BU40N at `0x3353c`; each saves, temporarily replaces and restores the word at the previously cited addresses.

A scan of halfword-aligned Thumb BL encodings across each full image found no direct BL to these three function entries. A nearby Thumb B scan and searches for the early BP50 entry as raw and several plausible relocated function-pointer literals likewise found no hits. These limited negative results do not exclude indirect dispatch, other relocation, ARM callers, or execution through a different entry. Its caller, whether it executes during UHD handling, and its ordering relative to G03 remain unresolved; consequently this routine cannot establish what consumes the field during a UHD read.
